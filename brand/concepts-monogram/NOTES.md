# Traasu monogram concepts

Three abstract monograms for Traasu. Each uses a 512 × 512 grid, one beam, one
stem and one weighing gesture, so the T and the balance share the same frame.
The colour files use exactly `#FDEFD6` background, `#731328` mark and `#FEC40B`
accent — the accent appears once, as an inlay inside the burgundy silhouette, so
the mono versions keep the same outline without it.

No mark uses strokes. The thinnest feature at 32 × 32 is 3 units; nothing depends
on a hairline, and every small file is a solid, printable silhouette.

## A — Hooked T

**Idea:** a heavy T whose crossbar overhangs into two short hangers, with a butter
pin set into the beam at the pivot — the balance frame reduced to one slab.

**Why it survives 16 px:** the mark is a single high-contrast T: 4-unit arms, a
6-unit stem and 4-unit hangers at 32 × 32 (2–3 px at 16). The accent is an inlay,
so it disappears into the beam in mono without changing the shape.

**Weakest point:** its scale reading is the most inferred of the three. At large
size the overhangs can read as a table or a squared bracket; only the centre pin
and the symmetry say "balance".

## B — Suspended T

**Idea:** a rounded T with two shallow pans hanging from long drops at the beam
ends and a butter pivot dot at the joint — a balance that keeps the T as its
frame.

**Why it survives 16 px:** beam and stem are 4–6 units at 32; the pans are solid
8 × 3-unit forms, so they close into two side tabs rather than vanishing. The
pivot dot is an inlay and is omitted in mono.

**Weakest point:** the pans read as small lamps or bells at large size, and the
four extra terminals make this the busiest silhouette. It is the least
monogram-like of the three.

## C — Weight T

**Idea:** a T with two solid disc pans hung from short strings and a bar base,
with a butter keystone in the beam at the pivot — a standing balance built out of
the letter.

**Why it survives 16 px:** every element is a solid block: 6-unit discs at 32, a
4-unit beam, 4-unit strings and a 4-unit base. The T stays legible even after the
discs and base soften, and the keystone is an inlay, so mono is the same
silhouette.

**Weakest point:** at 16 px the discs crowd the arm ends and can read as clubs or
ears, and the base pushes the mark slightly toward a trophy.

## Recommendation

Develop **C**. It is the only candidate that reads as a balance scale at 512
without explanation, and its weight sits in solid blocks — discs, beam and base —
rather than in thin hangs, so the 16 px and 1-bit versions degrade gracefully.
A is the better pure monogram if the balance reference is judged too oblique;
B needs its pans redrawn before it is more than a sketch.

## Rejected

- Literal pans with handles and chains: fussy at 16 px and below the 1.5-unit
  floor.
- A cream T cut out of a burgundy tile: the negative counterform closes up in
  1-bit thermal print.
- A thin pointer above the beam: read as an antenna or cross at 16 px and made
  the mark top-heavy.
- Arched or tapered crossbars: lost the clean T at 16 px.
